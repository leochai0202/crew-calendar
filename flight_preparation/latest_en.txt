Core threats:

Ningbo Lishe International Airport:
Terrain lies west and southwest of the airport, including a low isolated hill near the Runway 13 final approach. In adverse weather, aircraft arriving from the west must observe the sector minimum safe altitude and airway minimum safe altitude restrictions. If the Lishe NDB and Ningbo VOR are unavailable, the Runway 31 EOSID is unavailable. On the Runway 13 VOR/DME approach, the final approach track is offset by 4 degrees from the runway centreline. The Runway 13 VOR approach has a 5.7% descent gradient, equivalent to FPA3.26.

Military activity makes the airspace within the Ningbo ATC control area complex.

Do not select a by-ATC arrival before receiving ATC clearance. The QNH boundary between Ningbo Airport and the Shanghai terminal area overlaps; set the altimeter strictly in accordance with ATC instructions.

If ATC clears the aircraft direct to Point A, ensure the corresponding waypoint is correctly entered during approach preparation. Particular attention should be paid to energy management during a short-final visual approach. If visual reference to the runway cannot subsequently be maintained, radar vectors will be required. Due to terrain near Runway 31, radar vectoring may progressively compress the descent profile; therefore, manage speed and aircraft energy early to avoid an excessive-energy condition.

When the Runway 13 ILS approach is unavailable, use the RNP13 approach; the published procedure supports LNAV only.

The Runway 13 threshold is displaced by 150m; be alert to the resulting visual effect.

The B9 sign is on the runway side. After landing on Runway 13, ATC normally instructs aircraft to hold before B10 and then turn left at B9 to follow the follow-me vehicle. B9 is close to B10, whose sign is on the apron side, so B9 can easily be missed.

The ground markings at the L1 and B8 turnouts are unclear and may be mistaken for the L1 taxiway west of B8. Crew members are advised to exercise caution.

Special complex procedures: PBN single-engine departure procedures for Runways 31 and 13, and the traditional single-engine departure procedure for Runway 31.

Terrain is complex, and the Runway 13 ILS approach has a steep descent gradient of GP3.2.

Sanya Phoenix International Airport:
On the VOR08 approach to Runway 08, the arrival profile may be high; monitor the altitude-to-distance relationship.

When traffic inbound to Runway 26 is heavy, ATC may issue descent clearance late; reduce speed promptly and manage aircraft energy.

During the procedure turn, ATC may clear descent to establish on the glide path at 900m, whereas the published procedure establishes it at 700m. Coordinate with ATC to descend in accordance with the published procedure and establish on the glide path.

Sanya has ILS Y and ILS Z procedures. Confirm the active procedure from the broadcast in advance. ILS Z is normally used, but ATC may not specify Y or Z; verify the cleared procedure and avoid selecting the wrong one.

Turbulence may be encountered on final approach.

Taxi routes are complex. There are no taxiway centreline lights between Taxiways A3/A4, Taxiway A and the runway. When vacating the runway, monitor the pavement carefully, especially in rain at night, to avoid leaving the paved surface.

The PORAP-1N departure is commonly used from Runway 08. Clearance normally requires crossing PORAP at 7200m; performance can generally meet this requirement.

When military activity is present, a conventional departure may be assigned; observe the speed restriction at PORAP.

After departure, Sanya Area Control ATC may clear the aircraft to an RVSM level and require that level to be reached before NUMKU.
